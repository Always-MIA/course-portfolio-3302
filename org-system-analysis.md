# Organizing System Analysis: Science Museum

**Website:** https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/

---

# Introduction

The Science Museum website serves as a public knowledge base that provides access to scientific information, museum collections, exhibitions, educational resources, and historical artifacts. Its organizing system is designed to help visitors, researchers, students, and educators efficiently discover information through multiple navigation methods. Rather than presenting information as one large archive, the website organizes resources into meaningful categories and relationships that support both casual browsing and focused research.

---

# Primary Resources Being Organized

The primary resources organized by the Science Museum website include:

- Museum collection objects and artifacts
- Permanent and temporary exhibitions
- Historical scientific inventions
- Educational articles and learning resources
- Events and activities
- Stories about scientific discoveries and innovators
- Museum visitor information

For example, users can explore individual collection objects such as early computers, medical equipment, space technology, and transportation artifacts. Each object includes photographs, descriptions, historical context, and related materials that help explain its significance.

---

# Primary Interactions Supported

The website supports several important interactions depending on the user's purpose.

The first interaction is **research**. Students, educators, and researchers can search for specific scientific artifacts, historical topics, or inventors using the search feature.

The second interaction is **browsing**. Visitors can freely explore exhibitions, collections, stories, and educational content without knowing exactly what they are looking for.

The website also supports **learning and fact-checking** by providing curated historical information written by museum experts. Educational articles connect scientific concepts with real artifacts, making the site useful for classroom assignments and independent learning.

The website also supports **museum planning** by allowing visitors to find opening hours, ticket information, exhibitions, accessibility information, and upcoming events.

---

# Classification System

The Science Museum primarily uses a combination of **hierarchical** and **faceted** classification systems.

Its hierarchical organization is visible in the main navigation menu, which groups information into broad categories such as **Visit Us**, **Explore**, **Collection**, **Schools**, and **Events**. These major categories are then divided into increasingly specific pages.

The website also uses faceted organization within its collections. Users can filter resources by subject, time period, object type, or collection area, allowing them to narrow large numbers of resources into manageable groups.

This combination is highly effective because casual visitors can browse through categories while researchers can quickly locate very specific information using filters and search tools.

---

# Relationships Between Resources

Relationships between resources are represented in several ways throughout the website.

Each collection object contains hyperlinks to related artifacts, exhibitions, and historical topics. These connections encourage users to continue exploring similar resources without returning to the homepage.

Articles frequently reference related scientific discoveries, historical figures, or technologies through embedded hyperlinks.

The website also uses navigation menus, breadcrumbs, and internal links to help users understand where they are within the site's structure and easily move between related sections.

Search results often connect users to multiple resource types, including collection objects, educational articles, exhibitions, and events, creating additional relationships across different kinds of information.

---

# Conclusion

The Science Museum website demonstrates a well-designed organizing system that balances research, education, and public engagement. Its combination of hierarchical navigation, faceted filtering, search functionality, and extensive internal linking allows users with different goals to efficiently discover relevant information. By clearly organizing scientific collections while connecting related resources, the website creates an effective digital knowledge base that supports learning, exploration, and historical research for a wide audience.
